What this is for

You already know the business. Customers, jobs, stock, who paid, who owes you, who you can trust. What you may not have is a computer program that holds that — only accounting, and a lot of it still in your head.

This is not “you are behind on computers.” You are ahead on the work. I put one repeating loop in a box you can see and run.

  • You can see that loop on one screen.
  • The computer does the boring repeats.
  • You still decide the exceptions.
  • It still runs when I am gone.
  • You are not locked into a monthly bill you do not understand.

Three ways this usually starts

Paid discovery first: a few thousand dollars, a written brief in plain English. Then a fixed quote. Not hourly. Not “I’ll computerise the whole business.”

The office loop

About two weeks after the brief. Fixed price.

Enquiries, quotes, invoices, follow-up — wired into the accounting you already use. One repeating path, not a new empire.

What’s in your head

Fixed quote after the brief.

Prices, job rules, the bits only you remember — in one place you and a simple assistant can both use. You still have the last word.

On call

Only after handover. Only if you ask.

Small monthly for hosting and a check-in with a hours cap. New loops are a new quote. If you do not sign, I am gone and the system still runs.

Three dates on the first quote

  1. Start — we begin the loop we named.
  2. Done — it runs on your work, not a demo.
  3. Handover complete — you have clicked it yourself, on your own login, on a real job, without me in the room.

If you cannot run it, the job is not finished. I stay in the original price until you can, or we stop and do not pretend it shipped. Training is one real workflow, on your real work, until you can catch a bad output. A one-page guide per loop: what it does, what you click, what a good result looks like, and what to do when the three failures that actually happen turn up — including who to ring if it is the accountant’s software, not mine.

“One more thing” after handover is a new quote. Kitchen done; the deck is another job.

Worth a quote, or not

Write if you can name the leak

“Two days a week on this.” “We quote late and lose jobs.” “Only I know the prices.” Remote, scoped, written brief. Australia.

Not this job

A partner, equity, no budget, “just computerise everything,” on-site in the industrial suburbs, a phone call as the first step, or on-call IT with no retainer.

How I work with people who are not programmers

I sit with the work, use the thing myself first, throw out the bits that lie, and hand over something a colleague will actually click. Years of that — including mass-email tools at EBSCO that non-technical staff ran, with a human check before send.

I write the summary for someone who can think software and does not need to know programming. Clear Australian English. No fog. You own the logins. Running cost stays small because you are not renting a black box.

Name the leak

Write what repeats, what it costs in hours or dollars, and what only you know. I usually reply within 1–2 business days with whether it is a fit and what paid discovery would cover.
